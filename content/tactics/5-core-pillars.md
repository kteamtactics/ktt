Winning more games doesn't necessarily mean you're becoming a better player. Sometimes, you're just getting better at playing the same way. Real progress comes from understanding why a decision works, when it fails, and what alternatives you missed. Here are five pillars worth building on.

## Scoring

Kills are satisfying, but Victory Points win games. Before committing to an action, ask what it scores, what it denies, and what it costs you. In the end kills can stastfying and rewarding,

## Rules Knowledge

Knowing what your operatives can do is the starting point. Knowing exactly when and how they can do it is what creates opportunities. Knowing what the opponent wants and can do feels like cheating. Knowing how to prevent it is a super power.

## Activation Economy

Every activation is a resource. Moving, shooting or waiting can all be correct — the challenge is knowing which action gains you the most.

## Positioning

A powerful operative in the wrong place is still a wasted opportunity. Range, visibility, cover and objective control shape what is possible before the dice are even rolled.

## Reviewing your games

Don't just ask why you lost. Find the decision that changed the game in your favor or against you. What did you know at the time? What did you overlook? What would you do differently?

You don't need to master everything at once. Start by identifying one weakness, understand the rules behind it, and work on it deliberately. Which of these five pillars do you find hardest to master?
